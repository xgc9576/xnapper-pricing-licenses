## Which Xnapper license should you buy?

Buy Basic if you work on one Mac and don't expect a second one soon. The $29.99 covers everything the app does; the only thing you give up is a second activation.

Buy Personal if you have a laptop and a desktop, or if you're likely to replace your Mac within a couple of years. Unlinking an old machine and activating a new one is allowed through the license manager, so a single-device license doesn't tie you to one computer forever. Two activations just mean you never have to think about it.

Standard only makes sense if three Macs are in play today. The $25 gap over Personal buys exactly one more activation and nothing else.

Team is for a group that wants the purchase to sit on a company card and renew itself. A two-person studio should buy two Personal licenses for $109.98 once rather than pay $120 per device every two years.

## Who should skip Xnapper

Xnapper is a Mac screenshot and annotation tool, and macOS already ships with a screenshot utility that handles the basics. If all you do is grab a region and paste it into Slack, the built-in shortcut is free and already on your keyboard. Paying $29.99 to replace it only makes sense if you regularly spend time making captures look presentable before they go out.

The second group who should think twice is anyone working across platforms. The license is tied to Mac activations, and the iOS app is a separate purchase from the App Store that the desktop key doesn't cover. If your screenshots come from a phone as often as from a Mac, one license doesn't solve that.

Third, if you expect an app to keep improving year after year on a single payment, the update model will irritate you. You get twelve months of updates, then you stay on the version you have. That's a fair deal for software you use daily, but it isn't what everyone assumes a one-time purchase means.

## The fine print that catches people out

**Updates expire, the app doesn't.** Twelve months of updates come with the license. When they run out, you keep the last version you installed and nothing stops working. Renewing costs 60% of your original price: $17.99 on Basic, $32.99 on Personal, $47.99 on Standard. You can skip a year and renew later if a version worth paying for shows up.

**The iOS app is not included.** Xnapper's own FAQ states the license key can't be used in the iOS app, which has to be bought separately through the App Store. Budget for it if phone screenshots are part of your workflow.

**VAT depends on where you are.** The listed price may or may not include it, and the vendor points you to your receipt email if you need to claim it back.

**Moving between Macs takes a step.** You unlink the old device from the app's preferences or the license manager before activating a new one. It's a two-minute job, but it isn't automatic. Additional devices can be added later at a discounted price through the license manager.

**Directory scores deserve a second look.** iTechGuides gives Xnapper 4.8 and still ranks it 35th of 61 in its screen-capture category, on a page that openly states vendors can pay for position. The score is worth more than the ranking.

## Xnapper FAQ

**Is there a lifetime license?**

Yes, in the sense that matters. The license is perpetual, so you pay once and the version you have keeps working with no expiry date. It doesn't include lifetime updates, which is the part people mix up. Updates run for one year.

**Can I use one license on both my Mac and my iPhone?**

No. The desktop key doesn't work in the iOS app, which is sold separately on the App Store.

**What happens if I don't renew after the first year?**

The app keeps working. You stay on the last version released during your update window and can renew whenever you want at 40% off your original price.

**Can I get a refund?**

Within 30 days, no questions asked, by emailing the vendor's support address.

**Does the price include VAT?**

It depends on your country. The vendor's FAQ says the price may or may not include VAT and explains how to claim it back from your receipt.
